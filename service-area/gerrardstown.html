Paving in Gerrardstown, West Virginia

Gerrardstown is home base for A+ Paving & Landscaping, and these are our neighbors. The drives around here are rural Berkeley County lanes, often on a slope, often longer than a subdivision apron, and often in need of grading before anyone brings asphalt. Hillside ground and clay that holds water make a tough combination, and we see it in the yards we drive past every day.

Slope changes the base

A flat overlay on a hill just moves the water faster. On a Gerrardstown lane the first question is where the water goes when it leaves the pavement. If it runs down the center, it cuts a channel. If it dumps off the low side onto soft clay, the edge unravels. Grading has to cut and fill enough to put a crown on the lane and a ditch or a shed that does not wash the neighbor. Only then does a compacted stone base make sense. Paving over mud because the surface "looks close" is how a new drive fails in the first freeze.

Side-yard extensions need the same base. A strip of asphalt next to a sound drive still fails if it is sitting on topsoil. Temperature swings here open any crack that took on water. Fill those cracks before winter, and sealcoat in a dry late-summer or early-fall window when daytime air stays warm. We will not seal a lane that is still weeping from the clay bank beside it. The sealer will not bond, and on a slope it can track downhill.

County road, private lane, HOA

Many of these lanes meet a Berkeley County road at a culvert or a swell, not a city curb. The apron across that swell is the piece to confirm before it is paved. Check with Berkeley County, and with your HOA if the lane is shared or covenant-controlled. If the drive also needs a cut into the bank, that is excavation and grading, then driveway paving. We handle steeper hillside work in Berkeley Springs and flatter drives in Inwood and Martinsburg.

Garden edges and porch beds still have to let water past. See landscaping if the project includes the yard, not only the lane. Call for an estimate and walk the slope with us before the surface is chosen. Asphalt and tar and chip are both on the table for these lanes, and our asphalt versus tar and chip comparison lays out the difference.
