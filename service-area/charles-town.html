Paving in Charles Town, West Virginia

Charles Town is in Jefferson County, and the service-area page already describes the mix we see there: older properties and newer developments. That mix is the whole paving problem. A historic street may have a narrow apron, an older sidewalk, and drainage that was never designed for a wider drive. A new subdivision may have a standard curb cut and an HOA that cares what the drive looks like. Both still sit on clay-rich limestone-valley soil that holds water and heaves in a freeze.

Two kinds of apron

Do not treat them as the same permit question. Inside the city, or on a Jefferson County road outside it, the office you call is not Berkeley County. Check with the City of Charles Town or Jefferson County, and with the HOA, before a driveway apron is widened or rebuilt. On an older street, ask before you assume the apron can grow. On a new street, ask what the covenants say about material and width. This page is general guidance. It does not list a fee, a form number, or a deadline.

The private drive behind that apron still needs a base. An overlay hides a sound surface. It does not fix clay pumping up through alligator cracks. Water should leave the pavement instead of standing against a stone foundation, which is common on the older houses. Driveway paving covers the rebuild. Sealcoating, which we already illustrate with Charles Town work, is the maintenance step once the surface is sound: cracks filled, pavement dry, late summer or early fall, air above 50°F while it cures.

Hardscape on the same lots

Patios and walks are part of the Charles Town work we already describe, not a separate county. A patio tight to an old house has to sit so roof water and driveway water are not the same puddle. That is landscaping and hardscaping. Commercial stalls and shared lots, where they come up, are parking lots.

Jefferson County's other stated town page is Shepherdstown, where the lots we describe are flatter. Ranson is named on the service area page and does not have its own page. Contact us with the address and a note about whether the house is on an older street or in a newer section. That tells us which apron question to ask first.
